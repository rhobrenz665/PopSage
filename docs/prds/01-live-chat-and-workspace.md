# PRD 01 — Live chat and workspace

Status: Product baseline approved · Priority: v1 required · Related: [Product brief](README.md)

## Problem and outcome

Visitors and support teammates need a shared conversation that remains understandable and recoverable across refreshes, connection failures, and changes in who is responding. Success means a visitor can contact a business from its website and a teammate can reliably continue that conversation in the dashboard.

## Scope

Include workspace setup, verified email/password owner/team authentication through Neon Managed Better Auth, a website installation snippet, iframe widget, persistent conversation history, inbox, human messaging, and closing a conversation. PopSage authorizes workspace membership and roles on every protected action; authentication alone is insufficient. Exclude public signup, social/OAuth login, customer-support email delivery, ticket workflows, native apps, CRM integration, advanced permissions, and visitor attachments. Transactional authentication or invitation messages remain an onboarding dependency, not a support channel.

A workspace is one business support operation with shared knowledge and inbox across its allowed websites. Brands needing distinct answers use separate workspaces. Workspace owners control team admission; unrestricted public signup is deferred.

Approved defaults: anonymous visitors, text messages, one active conversation per visitor per workspace, and two workspace roles. Owners manage setup, knowledge, and membership; teammates read workspace conversations, claim, reply, and close. The same person may belong to multiple isolated workspaces. A visitor token is a scoped access credential, not a verified customer identity.

The host-page loader stores an opaque visitor-session handle in the embedding origin’s first-party browser storage and exchanges it for 15-minute iframe access credentials. Rotate the handle and expire it after 90 days of inactivity. Scope both to the visitor, workspace, and exact allowed origin. Do not rely on third-party iframe cookies/storage. Clearing site data creates a new visitor identity; no cross-origin, cross-browser, or cross-device continuity is promised. See [ADR 0008](../adr/0008-first-party-anonymous-visitor-sessions.md).

## User journey

1. After the platform operator admits the fictional training business (or approves a business for a future pilot), the owner signs in, creates a workspace, and configures the business name, support scope, and allowed website origins.
2. Owner sends a single-use, expiring email invitation to a specified teammate. After authenticating and accepting it, the teammate becomes a workspace member. Public self-registration is disabled. The owner then copies the widget installation snippet.
3. Visitor opens the installed widget and sees the business name, AI disclosure, and a way to request a human.
4. Visitor sends a message; the widget distinguishes sending, accepted, and failed states. The conversation appears in the workspace inbox.
5. A teammate opens the conversation, reviews history and handoff reason, claims it, and replies. The visitor sees the human’s identity and response.
6. A teammate closes the conversation. Approved: a later visitor message starts a new conversation while the earlier transcript remains available to that visitor and the workspace team until its retention period expires.

## Acceptance scenarios

- **Installation:** On a separate sample website with an allowed origin, a visitor can open the widget, send a message, and see a dashboard reply without refreshing either surface.
- **Isolation:** A workspace A teammate or visitor cannot read, post to, or subscribe to workspace B conversations by changing identifiers. Visitor A cannot access visitor B in the same workspace.
- **Recovery:** After a connection drop following server acceptance but before client acknowledgment, retry/reconnect shows the message once and restores subsequent messages in order.
- **Concurrent claim:** Two teammates claim at the same time; exactly one succeeds. The other sees the current owner and cannot reply as owner without an explicit transfer.
- **Revocation:** A removed teammate loses access to new messages and actions, including an already-open live connection.
- **Invitation isolation:** A 72-hour invitation works once for its addressed account/workspace. Expired, revoked, replaced, consumed, wrong-account, and wrong-workspace attempts fail without creating membership.
- **Visitor bootstrap:** Supported browsers recover the same visitor from the first-party handle on the same origin. A different origin, cleared storage, invalid handle, or mismatched iframe bootstrap cannot access the earlier visitor’s transcript.
- **Credential replay:** After a successful handle rotation, replaying the replaced handle fails and does not invalidate or expose the current session. A validly signed access token also fails after current session/origin revocation.
- **Outbox recovery:** Force PartyServer publication to fail after a successful database commit. The message remains durable, the dispatcher later republishes it, consumers display it once, and cursor recovery preserves order. Force the database transaction to roll back and confirm no event becomes durable or visible.
- **Fast-path duplication:** Let post-commit publication succeed but withhold its acknowledgment so the dispatcher publishes the same event again. Widget/dashboard apply the stable event ID and sequence once.
- **Gap recovery:** Deliver sequence N+1 before N. The client does not apply it out of order; it fetches persisted missing state/events and resumes from a contiguous cursor.
- **Immutable transcript:** Attempt to edit or selectively delete an accepted message through visitor, teammate, owner, operator, and direct application endpoints; all fail. A correction appears as a new ordered message.
- **Poison event:** Repeatedly fail one event. Later events in that stream do not overtake it, one metadata-only alert remains open, and recovery does not remove the corresponding durable message.
- **Teammate removal:** Remove a teammate with open connections and assigned conversations. Access ends immediately across HTTP/realtime, historical sender labels remain understandable, and memberships in another workspace remain unaffected.
- **Owner transfer:** The owner cannot leave without a completed accepted transfer. A nonmember, pending invitee, removed teammate, or teammate who has not accepted cannot become owner.
- **Workspace deletion:** Wrong confirmation or stale reauthentication fails. Correct confirmation disables all workspace paths immediately, cancels/rejects pending work, and produces idempotent cleanup obligations without affecting another workspace.
- **Compacted cursor:** Reconnect with a cursor older than seven days. The authorized client receives a consistent snapshot and new cursor, without losing accepted transcript messages or applying newer events twice.
- **Account deletion:** An owner with unresolved workspaces cannot delete their account. After transfer/deletion, account deletion immediately revokes every access path, removes the auth profile within 24 hours, anonymizes retained sender display names, and leaves accepted content unchanged.
- **Input validation:** Empty, whitespace-only, malformed, and 4,001-character messages persist nothing, call no AI capability, remain editable, and do not reset inactivity. Meaningful Unicode at or below 4,000 characters remains intact.
- **Abuse limits:** The eleventh session message or thirty-first origin message inside the applicable minute is rejected before AI work with a retry time. Another workspace/origin is unaffected; changing a claimed customer identity does not bypass the anonymous session boundary.
- **Sensitive reauthentication:** Stale authentication cannot delete a workspace/account, transfer ownership, or grant transcript access. MFA absence in training does not weaken this reauthentication requirement.
- **Authentication boundary:** A verified account without membership cannot access a workspace. Public signup and OAuth paths are absent. Wrong-email invitation acceptance and development-email recovery for an unrecognized identity do not create membership.
- **Origin matching:** Allow `https://support.example.com` and deny `http://support.example.com`, `https://support.example.com.evil.test`, wildcard hosts, embedded credentials, paths, queries, fragments, Unicode lookalikes, and port mismatches. Permit explicitly configured HTTP localhost only in development.
- **Room isolation:** A workspace inbox subscriber sees conversation summaries/state but no message body. A teammate, visitor, or operator lacking conversation authorization cannot connect to its room or fetch missed events.
- **RLS isolation:** With application filters deliberately omitted in a test path, Postgres policies still deny cross-workspace access. Missing transaction context denies access, and a background job cannot act outside its explicit workspace/job scope.
- **Human-only operation:** With AI disabled or unavailable, a visitor can still send messages and receive human replies.
- **Closed conversation:** A visitor sees closure; sending again follows the defined new-conversation behavior without losing the new message.

## Conversation lifecycle and retention

These rules apply to the training environment and remain the baseline for any future pilot:

1. **Open:** AI-active, waiting-for-human, and human-active conversations are eligible for inactivity closure. Measure inactivity from the last accepted, persisted visitor, AI, or human conversational message; initialize it at conversation creation if empty. System notices, retries of the same message, reads, and background events do not reset the clock.
2. **Automatic closure:** After 30 days of inactivity, close the conversation once and record `inactivity` as the closure reason. An unanswered visitor request is marked abandoned rather than resolved. An acknowledgment, refusal, or waiting notice is not proof the request was answered. Other inactivity closures have an unconfirmed outcome unless resolution was already established; closure alone never establishes resolution.
3. **Closed:** Retain the historical transcript for 30 days from closure. Later events do not extend this deadline. Closure invalidates pending AI output and revokes the old conversation's reply channel.
4. **New message:** A visitor message after closure starts a new conversation using the existing authorized visitor session when still valid. It does not reopen the old conversation or reset its retention clock. A human reply targeting a closed conversation fails clearly rather than reopening it silently.
5. **Concurrent activity:** Acceptance and automatic closure must agree atomically on the current activity/state. If a new message is accepted first, it resets inactivity and the stale closure attempt does nothing. If closure wins, a visitor send is routed to a new conversation exactly once. Never acknowledge a message that is silently discarded. Late AI results must not create new conversations or reset inactivity.
6. **Expired:** After 30 days closed, the transcript is unavailable to visitors, teammates, and troubleshooting access. A visitor may start a fresh conversation after session validation; expiry must not resurrect old content. Primary cleanup follows the 24-hour target below; provider-managed backup expiry remains to be documented.

Logically expired transcripts and visitor sessions are inaccessible immediately. Retryable, idempotent Trigger.dev jobs remove them from primary application storage within 24 hours; cleanup failure raises an operational error without restoring access. Provider-managed backup, restore-history, log, and replica retention must be documented separately. See [ADR 0009](../adr/0009-logical-expiry-and-physical-cleanup.md).

Knowledge remains until owner removal and follows PRD 02's retrieval-exclusion rules. Closing, abandonment, resolution, and expiry are distinct concepts; use separate outcome/reason metadata rather than treating all closed conversations as resolved.

Acceptance checks: simulate both 30-day boundaries without waiting in real time; prove system events do not extend retention; race closure against visitor send and AI completion; retry closure/cleanup safely; verify expired content is unavailable through every access path. Include an unanswered waiting conversation and a previously answered but unconfirmed conversation to ensure neither is falsely counted as resolved.

Cleanup acceptance checks: retry transient failures inside the 24-hour window; when the deadline is missed, emit one deduplicated metadata-only alert in the operator-only dashboard and structured logs containing record type, workspace, job, deadline, and failure category. Keep it open until cleanup succeeds or an operator records resolution. Confirm it contains no transcript/document content and access remains denied.

## Troubleshooting access

Platform operators have no automatic right to read workspace transcripts. Explicit workspace-owner authorization must identify the operator, permitted workspace/conversations, purpose, and bounded access window. Record the reason, grant, use, expiry, and revocation in an audit trail without copying transcript contents into that trail. Access is denied outside the grant and cannot cross tenant boundaries or override transcript expiry. A project owner may play multiple fictional roles during training, but tests must use distinct role identities to prove there is no operator bypass. Provider-console/database privileged access must also be considered when designing the operational controls; an application-only check is insufficient.

Acceptance checks: deny an operator without a grant; allow only its scoped transcripts; deny other conversations/workspaces and deny access after grant expiry or owner revocation. Enforce a maximum 24-hour grant, terminate access earlier on transcript expiry, and retain content-free grant/use/revocation audit metadata for 90 days.

## Measurement and dependencies

Approved target: p95 accepted-message-to-connected-recipient display ≤1 second at the documented training load, with the original higher-load goal retained for later validation. All reconnect, duplicate-send, ownership, and isolation scenarios must pass in training and again before a real pilot. Depend on workspace/session authorization, durable message storage, the transactional outbox, PartyServer/PartySocket, and the shared state contract. Postgres remains authoritative; realtime transport failure or quota exhaustion must not erase accepted messages. See [ADR 0006](../adr/0006-partyserver-for-realtime.md), [ADR 0010](../adr/0010-transactional-outbox-for-realtime-events.md), and [ADR 0011](../adr/0011-defense-in-depth-tenant-isolation.md).

Exact token claims/key rotation, provider backup schedules, outbox/database mechanics, email transport configuration, and other implementation mechanics remain engineering validation; they do not reopen product scope unless evidence reveals a material conflict.
